Algorithm Overview and Current Experimental Results

1. Overall Idea

The overall goal is to address open-set graph anomaly detection by improving the model's ability to capture normal graph patterns rather than relying primarily on the limited anomaly types observed during training. In this setting, the training data contains a small number of seen anomalies, while the test data contains both seen and unseen anomalies. A model that overfits to the seen anomaly types may fail when the anomaly distribution changes.

The general idea is to learn a normality-oriented graph representation and scoring mechanism. The model is encouraged to characterize regular attribute and structural patterns in the graph, and nodes that are less consistent with these learned normal patterns are assigned higher anomaly scores. The same scoring principle is applied to both seen and unseen anomalies, so the inference procedure does not depend on whether a test anomaly belongs to a known anomaly type.

This formulation can be interpreted as an open-set risk control strategy. Instead of treating the observed anomaly labels as a complete description of the anomaly space, the method emphasizes the modeling of normal graph behavior and uses deviations from normality as the basis for anomaly detection.

2. Datasets and Protocols

The current experiments cover Photo, Computers, CS, ogbn-arxiv, and YelpChi.

Photo, Computers, and CS are multi-class graph datasets. The experiments follow the nsreg_multiclass protocol, where low-frequency classes are treated as anomaly classes and high-frequency classes are treated as normal classes. In each trial, one anomaly class is selected as the seen anomaly class, and the remaining anomaly classes are treated as unseen anomaly classes.

ogbn-arxiv is a large-scale OGB citation graph with about 169K nodes, 1.17M edges, 128-dimensional node features, and 40 classes. The experiment selects low-frequency classes with proportions between 3% and 5% as anomaly classes. The selected candidate anomaly classes are [4, 8, 10, 34], and each class is rotated as the seen anomaly class.

YelpChi is a binary fraud detection dataset and does not contain natural anomaly subclasses. Therefore, the current Yelp experiment uses locally constructed anomaly subclasses. These results should be interpreted only as a protocol pressure test and should not be claimed as officially comparable SOTA results.

3. Current Main Experimental Results

3.1 Results on Photo, Computers, and CS

Under the PyG multi-class protocol, the averaged results over 16 trials are as follows:

Method	All AUROC	All AUPRC	Unseen AUROC	Unseen AUPRC	Unseen FNR
Supervised GCN	0.7651	0.5674	0.6713	0.3426	0.9324
GAE-GCN feat+edge	0.6420	0.2842	0.6304	0.2487	0.3735
NoCo-GB feature+edge	0.7283	0.3852	0.6661	0.2775	0.5669
NoCo-GB feature+edge + normal-pair	0.7637	0.4618	0.7010	0.3025	0.5756
NoCo-GB code+feature+edge + normal-pair	0.7963	0.5464	0.7357	0.3352	0.6081

These results show that the final NoCo-GB variant achieves the strongest performance among the compared internal variants. It improves All AUROC from 0.7283 to 0.7963 and Unseen AUROC from 0.6661 to 0.7357 compared with the feature+edge version.

Photo has an Unseen AUPRC of 0.1366, indicating that precision remains limited under low anomaly-rate conditions.

On ogbn-arxiv, the final code+edge version achieves All AUROC 0.6870, Unseen AUROC 0.6251, and Unseen AUPRC 0.1817. Compared with the public DEMO paper arxiv target, NoCo-GB improves All AUROC and Unseen AUROC, and it slightly exceeds the Unseen AUPRC target.

The per-unseen-class AUROC results are:

Class 34 is the most difficult unseen class, with AUROC around 0.53 to 0.54. The code+edge variant improves Class 8 most significantly, from 0.6012 to 0.6514.
